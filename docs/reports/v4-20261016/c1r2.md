# V4-C1r2 data revision experiment

Completed 2026-10-16 on the same rented training instance as C1: 11 passes over the conversion set, 42,240 updates, stopped normally by the conversion-validation plateau. The best Standard conditional score is 473/728 at epoch 5, step 19,200; the last epoch scores 467/728. Both remain below V4-B (498/728) and V2.1 (505/728). The experiment keeps the C1 task protocol and revises the conversion data and the LM retention policy. It is not a C2 ranking or teacher-distillation experiment.

## Changes and scope

The first C1 run triggered the LM retention guard at the end of epoch 2, with a full conditional score of 425/728. The same weights score 486/728 as a plain LM and 461/728 with an empty READ. A data investigation found that the training conversion fragments were much shorter than real inputs and covered proper nouns, punctuation and digits poorly.

C1r2 starts from the same V4-B step 584000 checkpoint with a fresh AdamW, new data and output directories. The model stays fixed: 8K vocabulary, 15,070,848 parameters, context 128. BF16, compilation, batch 256 conversion + 256 LM, loss 0.7/0.3, learning rate 1e-4→1e-5, warmup 1%, and at most 12 passes over the conversion set are unchanged. The different example count changes the total steps and the absolute warmup steps.

An auxiliary change is `lm_retention_policy = "monitor"`: full LM BPC, the original 1% eligibility check and consecutive-deterioration counts are still recorded, but no longer stop training or veto rerank checkpoints. `best-rerank.pt`/`best.pt` are chosen by the existing order of Standard correct count, conversion NLL and step; their LM retention eligibility must be read from the matching full evaluation record, not inferred from the file name. The conversion plateau rule is unchanged: 0.2% relative cumulative improvement, patience 3.

This run is therefore not a strict single-variable test of the data alone; data, the actual exposure under the budget, and the stopping/selection policy are distinguished explicitly in the records.

## Data sources and rules

Parent sentences are selected by parent hash from the reviewed C1 list: training parents with hash mod 100 below 25 get extended views, 25–34 keep their reviewed short views, the rest are not used. Validation parents below 72 get extended views; the rest keep short views.

Extended views reread the original frozen TXT and confirm their source by byte offset, parent hash and old span. Within a bounded window around the parent's original target, a continuous 8–48 character notation is labeled, with a reading of at most 72 characters, still within the fixed 128 prediction positions after encoding. MeCab/UniDic and Sudachi must give the same reading on shared source boundaries. Known proper nouns are no longer excluded outright, but fragments that are unknown or where the two readings disagree are rejected. Kana keeps keyboard orthography; digits, ASCII and punctuation stay as directly typed text; READ applies the same NFKC and katakana-to-hiragana normalization as inference. Japanese spoken readings of digits are not guessed. The existing single-character compound-boundary and 「家」 ambiguity guards remain.

Training excludes every original validation parent hash, the frozen conversion-validation tasks and encoded sequences, and known non-blind development/regression items by exact identity. No development answers were used to add samples or change labels; test/blind was not read. Two-dictionary labels are still silver, not native-speaker gold, and no complete document- or meaning-level non-overlap is claimed. The inherited `stratum` field records the sampling stratum of the original C1 parent view; the revised actual distribution is the histogram in this run's manifest.

The best conditional score, against AzooKey, fixes 86 items and breaks 66, net +20; against the first C1 run's epoch 2 checkpoint, +92/−44, net +48; against V4-B, +53/−78, net −25. JMultiWOZ is 206/245 (V4-B 201); WRIME is 267/483 (V4-B 297). The main gap is still WRIME, and the model cannot replace the deployment.

These comparisons use exactly the same 728 frozen candidate sets and references. The best epoch was selected on that development set, so the result is exploratory, not an independent test or a confirmatory statistical conclusion. Attributing all of 473−425 to any single data change is not valid, because training exposure and the stopping policy changed too.

On the training machine, `best-rerank.pt`/`best.pt` are epoch 5, `best-conversion.pt` epoch 8, and `best-lm.pt`/`last.pt` epoch 11. The raw summary, per-epoch scores and download identities are kept locally in `outputs/evaluation-v4-c1r2-20261016/`.

## Targeted diagnosis of the best checkpoint

`best-rerank.pt` was downloaded and verified (SHA256 `1db399d5a5002712e3dccced993de4aa5abf5cfd365d605c4b7bc3fc0a4801e4`, checkpoint step 19,200). Locally in FP32, on the original fixed 728 candidate sets, with zero training updates:

| Scoring path | Correct |
| --- | ---: |
| V4-B plain LM, existing result | 498/728 |
| C1r2 same weights, plain LM | 478/728 |
| C1r2 full CTX/READ conditional | 473/728 |
| C1r2 empty-READ conditional | 469/728 |
| C1r2 empty-CTX conditional | 468/728 |

The full conditional score reproduces the remote 473/728, and no path falls back on the window. READ adds only 4 items net in this run, not enough to show an independent, stable IME gain; empty READ makes up about 10% of training and is not an independently trained no-reading control.

For the original C1 weights, plain LM/full conditional scores were 486/425, so the conditional interface cost −61 net; after the revision they are 478/473, a gap of only −5. This supports the reading that the conditional data revision helps, but this run's plain LM ranking is still 20 items behind V4-B, so the drift of language ranking under conversion supervision remains. The scoring paths are not a linear causal decomposition, and 20/25 cannot be read as the exact contribution of any factor.

The revision restored several previously wrong 「札幌」「名古屋」 notations, but personal names and homophone choices still go wrong; widening generic conversion-fragment coverage alone did not fix all ranking problems. Given that the ranking peak (epoch 5) and the conversion-loss minimum (epoch 8) come apart, there is no clear case for training longer on the original conversion objective. A ranking objective on the training set's actual candidates, with learning rate and LM replay controlled independently, looks more worth studying; this is a suggestion, not a verified result.
